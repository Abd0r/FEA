FEA V3 M12 fabrication throughput
Patterning and areal rates are CITED from Randall 2018. The years follow.

[SCENARIO 1] count what actually has to be patterned
  clusters in V2's claim        : 1.130e+14
  clusters in our 5.000e-01 cm^2 design   : 1.875e+13
  atoms per cluster             : 5.000e+00
  atoms requiring a pulse       : 5.650e+14
  equivalent pulses (1 H each)  : 5.650e+14

  Reviewer 4 said 10^14 clusters. The pulse count is 5.7 x 10^14, because each cluster
  needs its atoms placed individually. Pathway atoms would add more and are
  not yet counted here, so this is a floor on the patterning work.

[SCENARIO 2] wall-clock patterning time from cited rates
  rate (atoms/s)   seconds          years
         1.0e+02        5.6e+12        1.8e+05
         1.0e+03        5.6e+11        1.8e+04
         1.0e+04        5.6e+10        1.8e+03
         1.0e+05        5.6e+09        1.8e+02
         1.0e+06        5.6e+08        1.8e+01

  cited rate 10000 atoms/s gives 1790.38 YEARS for one die, serial, one tip.
  Reviewer 4 said a 3 cm^2 die would take years. Confirmed for ONE tip: even
  at 1e6 atoms/s a single tip needs 17.9 years.
  this is a serial figure. It does not mean fabrication is impossible, because
  parallel scanning-probe arrays remove the multiplication. See SCENARIO 3.

  CITED areal throughput (Randall 2018): 0.053 um^2/h per tip
  die area                         : 300000000.00 um^2
  serial time by areal throughput  : 645719.53 YEARS
  ratio to the atom-count figure   : 360.7x worse
  the atom count understates the work by 361x, because the tip must sweep empty lattice it never writes.
  so the governing single-tip number for a 3 cm^2 die is 645720 years, not 1790.
  label: both rates CITED from Randall 2018. Atom count exact at 5.65e14.

[SCENARIO 3] parallel scanning-probe arrays make this countable, not impossible
  rate atoms/s   tips for 1.0 yr   tips for 0.25 yr
       1.0e+04          1.8e+03            7.2e+03
       1.0e+05          1.8e+02            7.2e+02
       1.0e+06          1.8e+01            7.2e+01

  at the cited 10000 atoms/s, one die in one year needs 1791 parallel tips.
  at an optimistic 1e6 atoms/s it needs only 18 tips.

  CITED scanner densities (Randall 2018 results table):
    electrostatic 1000:1 10101 /cm^2    electrothermal 200:1 37037 /cm^2
    demonstrated 3-DoF electrostatic device (Bell Labs) 2500 /cm^2
  wafer figures in the paper confirm 10 101 /cm^2: 65 168 per 1 in^2,
  793 330 per 100 mm wafer, 7 139 970 per 300 mm wafer.
  tips this die receives at that density : 30303 to 111111
  resulting wall clock                    : 21.31 to 5.81 YEARS

  so at the paper's OWN proposed density a 3 cm^2 die still takes 5.8 to 21.3 years.
  Reviewer 4's "would take years" holds on the cited numbers.
  the quantity that actually decides feasibility is tip-to-tip registration.
  one tip is slow but accurately placed. 1791.0 tips must all land on the
  same 1.15 nm lattice, and no cited source gives that registration error.
  label: rates CITED from Randall 2018. Achieved registration error OPEN.

[SCENARIO 4] defect rate multiplies the work, it does not remove it
  V2 hard-defect fraction        : 0.0500
  extra clusters to route around : 0.0526 (5.3%)
  single-pass time, no spares    : 1790.377 yr
  time including spare clusters  : 1884.607 yr
  naive whole-die yield at 5%    : 0.000e+00
  (V2 instead assumes boot-time defect mapping rescues the die.)

  finding: defect routing reduces scrapped die count but never reduces the
  atom-by-atom patterning work. Throughput and yield are separate problems and
  V2 addresses neither with a number.
  label: defect fraction from V2, rates cited from Randall 2018, yield naive on purpose
  to show that boot-time mapping is an assumption rather than a yield result.

[SCENARIO 5] what Reviewer 4's "pathway toward high-throughput" would require
  unknown or unquantified today:
    1. depassivation rate per tip   (CITED 10000 atoms/s, Randall 2018)
    2. parallel tip density         (CITED electrostatic 10101, electrothermal 37037/cm^2)
    3. tip lattice alignment         (CITED requirement 0.1 nm; achieved multi-tip error uncited)
    4. defect rate after patterning (V2 assumes 5%, uncited)
    5. DBW pathway atom count       (not counted in this module at all)

  so the honest statement for the manuscript is not "well-understood
  engineering challenge" but: at the cited 10000 atoms/s one serial tip needs 1790.4 years by atom count and 645720 by areal raster.
  Table II nominal exposure is 9.6 ms/atom, i.e. 104.2 atoms/s, consistent with the cited rate.
  the parallel tool's own control electronics are cited at 30.0 mW per scanner, about 303 W/cm^2. That is tool power, not FEA chip power.
  what remains uncited is the achieved multi-tip registration error, not the
  rate or the density. See SCENARIO 6 for the improvement actually needed.
  label: OPEN. This module quantifies the gap. It does not close it.

[SCENARIO 6] how much parallel improvement actually closes the gap
  target            um^2/h needed    tips needed   density /cm^2  gain vs cited
  1-year die                34223         645720          215240      5.81x
  3-month die              136893        2582878          860959     23.25x

  a one-year 3 cm^2 die needs 21.52e4 tips/cm^2.
  that is 5.81x the cited ELECTROTHERMAL density (37037.00/cm^2) and 21.31x the cited ELECTROSTATIC one (10101.00/cm^2).
  against the demonstrated 2500.00/cm^2 device it is 86.10x.
  the same factor can come from per-tip rate instead of tip count, because
  Randall 2018 argues parallel scaling is LINEAR, not exponential like the
  e-beam case that defeated parallelism.

  finding: the gap is 5.8x to 21.3x over the paper's projected densities, and 86.1x over what has actually been built.
  a 6-21x gain in tip density OR per-tip rate reaches a one-year die. That is a
  roadmap target, not an impossibility.
  the harder half stays atomic-precision registration across the array, which
  needs a demonstration rather than a scaling argument.
  label: gains derived from cited 2018 values. Rate-of-improvement uncited.
