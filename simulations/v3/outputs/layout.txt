FEA V3 M15 fabric layout as a function of die area
Die area is an input. Geometry comes from Block pitch. Widths are declared.

[SCENARIO 1] Zone geometry follows from Block pitch, not from chip size
  Block pitch                 : 1.1500 nm
  Blocks per Zone side        : 256
  Zone side                   : 294.4 nm (0.02944 um)
  Zone area                   : 8.667e-10 cm^2
  Blocks per Zone             : 65536
  a Zone is a ~300 nm object. Chip size does not change Zone geometry, only
  how many of them are tiled and how far apart the far ones sit.

[SCENARIO 2] layout as a function of die area
  die cm^2   side cm        zones       grid array cm    slack  FZC cm^2      bnd max hops
  1           1.0000    572220541      23921   0.7071   1.4142    0.0040    8.606%      47840

  10          3.1623   5722205410      75645   2.2361   1.4142    0.0405    2.763%     151289

  data cells from this module's Zone tiling : 1.4879 cm^2
  cells from V2's stated 1.13e14 Blocks    : 1.4944 cm^2
  disagreement between the two routes       : 0.440 %
  Zone total including the FZC strip       : 1.5000 cm^2 of 1.5000 cm^2 budgeted
  routing slack                : 1.4142 = sqrt(2.0000) BY CONSTRUCTION, an identity, not a measurement.
  note: data_tiled cancels block_area (zones already divide by it), so the
  check above is die-geometry occupancy versus V2's stated count x block
  area, not two pitch-derived routes. Claiming independent pitch routes was
  wrong; the comparison is still a real die-versus-claim test.
  zones scale linearly: 3.0x area -> 3.0x zones, and 10.0x at 10 cm^2.
  worst-case Slingshot hops grow sub-linearly: 3.162x for 10.000x the area, because hops follow the grid side, not the area.
  boundary ring share FALLS as the die grows: 8.6% at 1 cm^2 -> 5.0% at 3 cm^2 -> 2.8% at 10 cm^2.
  declared ring 22.000 mm costs 5.016% of V2's 3 cm^2 die but 12.058% of our 0.5 cm^2 design die.
  the width is fixed because boundary pads do not shrink, so a SMALLER die
  pays a LARGER share for I-O. Shrinking the die to ease fabrication costs
  this, and it is budgeted rather than hidden.
  so a bigger chip amortises the boundary ring, which is why the ratio is
  perimeter over area: it falls as 1/sqrt(area).

[SCENARIO 3] clock feeds and worst-case route length
  Zones                       : 1716661623
  Zone grid side              : 41433 Zones
  clock feeds at 1 per 256 Zones  : 6705710

  a Zone at one corner talking to the opposite corner crosses about 82863 hops.
  that number is what turns M9's local cycle into a system latency, and it
  scales as sqrt(die area), not linearly with Zone count.
  label: geometry derived from pitch, widths and grain DECLARED. Not a drawing.

[SCENARIO 4] capacity options: every structure must live inside the die
  V2: Block = 3a x 3a = 1.15 x 1.15 nm^2, practical density 3.77e13 cm^-2
  after 2x routing. Cells taken from V2's stated 1.13e14 Block count:
    Block cells             : 1.4944 cm^2
    routing (V2's own 2x)   : 1.4944 cm^2
    area V2 claims outright : 2.9888 cm^2  = 99.6% of a 3.0 cm^2 die

  option                          support cm^2   routing   cells cm^2   capacity   area needed   fits?
  A  V2 as published                   0.0000      2.0      1.5000    14.18 TB       2.9888   yes
      basis: assumes PDN, boundary and control are free
  B  FZC only                          0.0121      2.0      1.4939    14.12 TB       3.0010   NO
      basis: reserve control, still free PDN and I/O
  C  FZC + PDN 5%                      0.1621      2.0      1.4189    13.41 TB       3.1510   NO
      basis: cheaper power grid, no boundary ring
  D  FZC + PDN 10% + boundary          0.4626      2.0      1.2687    11.99 TB       3.4515   NO
      basis: every declared structure paid
  E  D but routing 1.5x                0.4626      1.5      1.6916    15.99 TB       2.7043   yes
      basis: needs justification for lower routing

  A needs 2.9888 cm^2 of a 3.0000 cm^2 die -> fits, with 0.0112 cm^2 left over.
  D needs 3.4515 cm^2 of a 3.0000 cm^2 die -> DOES NOT FIT, short by 0.4515 cm^2 (15.0%).

  D is the physically correct reading: the die is a fixed 3.0 cm^2, so cells,
  routing, FZC, PDN and the boundary ring must all come out of it. That
  takes capacity from 14.2 TB to 12.0 TB.
  A is not an option in the physical sense. It is what you get only if PDN,
  boundary I/O and control genuinely cost zero area, which they do not.
  E is the only route back above 14.1 TB, and it requires justifying a routing
  factor below the 2x V2 itself states.
  FZC alone needs 0.0121 cm^2 against V2's residual of 0.0112 cm^2, so it barely tips option B over (0.0% of die). But FZC is only 2.6% of the support term: PDN and the boundary ring are 97.4% of it. FZC is the smallest term, and it is not what drives the
  shortfall. V2's published numbers simply leave almost no headroom.
  label: cell area from V2's stated Block count, feasibility checked against the
  die. The three identities that used to sit here were removed: slack and the
  budget total were true by construction, not by measurement.
